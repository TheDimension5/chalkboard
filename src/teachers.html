Teacher's guide

One Rule for Everything

Missions and discussion questions for every chapter of the interactive page, at four reading bands. Print this, or keep it open on a second screen while the class works through the simulations.

How to use it

  • Pick the band that matches your class, then open the page at that level using the link under the band heading. The page remembers the level.
  • Each chapter has one or more missions inside the simulation. The page checks them off automatically and keeps a running count at the top.
  • Use the discussion questions after the missions, in pairs or as a whole class. Many have no single right answer; the goal is to make students say what they saw.
  • On the Kids and 8th grade levels, a Read to me button reads each chapter aloud with the browser's built-in voice.
  • Everything runs in the browser on phones, tablets, and laptops. No account, no installs.

Missions

  • Drag the star and the magnet close enough to ask the question.

1 · Two kinds of pull

Missions

  • Drop the apple.
  • Pick up all six paper clips with the magnet.

Discussion questions

  1. What are two things that can pull without touching?
  2. Why doesn't the paper clip jump until the magnet gets close?
  3. The apple falls and the paper clip jumps. Do you think it is the same kind of pull? Why or why not?

2 · Gravity is a dent

Missions

  • Get a marble to go around the Sun three times.

Discussion questions

  1. Why does a marble curve toward the heavy ball on a stretchy sheet?
  2. What happens to the marbles when you make the Sun heavier?
  3. The clock near the Sun falls behind. What do you think that would be like for a person standing there?

2 · Time runs slow

Missions

  • Drag the clock toward the Sun until it falls 5 ticks behind.

3 · Magnets have invisible hair

Missions

  • Add a third charge and watch the hair change.

Discussion questions

  1. What does the compass tell you about the invisible hair?
  2. Where is the hair thickest? Where is it thinnest?
  3. Does the field make a dent in the grid the way the Sun did? What is different?

4 · Einstein's wish

Missions

  • Slide Einstein's wish all the way to 100%.

Discussion questions

  1. What is the difference between a shape of the sheet and a pattern on the sheet?
  2. What did Einstein wish were true?
  3. If his wish came true, how many rulebooks would there be?

5 · A hidden tiny direction

Missions

  • Zoom in until the ant can walk around the tube.

Discussion questions

  1. Why does the hose look like a line from far away?
  2. What can the ant do that a giant cannot?
  3. If the loop is at every point in space, why can't we see it?

6 · Twisted space

Missions

  • Turn the dent all the way down and the twist all the way up.

Discussion questions

  1. What is the difference between a dent and a twist?
  2. Which way do marbles go when there is only twist?
  3. Why did newspapers get so excited before Einstein was finished?

7 · The lopsided ruler

Missions

  • Make the two rulers disagree by more than 5 steps.

Discussion questions

  1. How can a ruler count differently in two directions?
  2. Which part of the count did Einstein call gravity?
  3. Why do you think Einstein kept working on this until the end of his life?

8 · What we know now

Missions

  • Push the forces together.

Discussion questions

  1. How many pulls do scientists know about today?
  2. Which pull will not join the others?
  3. Why was Einstein trying to solve a puzzle with pieces missing?

Missions

  • Launch one + marble and one − marble.

Discussion questions

  1. What are the four things a winning rule has to do?
  2. Why can't scientists just look at very tiny things to find the answer?

Missions

Discussion questions

  1. What happened to the marbles when you made the pull fade faster?

  2. A marble in a stretched-out orbit speeds up and slows down. Where is it fastest, and why?
  3. Explain why the near clock runs slow, and how the bending wavefronts are related to falling.

Start: One Rule for Everything

Missions

  • Drag the star and the magnet close enough to ask the question.

1 · Two kinds of pull

Missions

  • Drop the apple.
  • Pick up all six paper clips with the magnet.
  • Drop both apples and watch them land at the same time.

Discussion questions

  1. Explain Einstein's 'marble and wood' remark and what it implies about the Einstein–Maxwell system.
  2. Gravity couples universally to the stress-energy tensor; electromagnetism couples to a conserved current with body-dependent charge-to-mass ratio. What would a geometric unification need to explain about this asymmetry?
  3. How did Weyl's 1918 scale-gauge proposal fail, and how did its 1929 reinterpretation succeed?

2 · Gravity is a dent

Missions

  • Get a marble to go around the Sun three times.
  • Throw a marble (drag and let go) so it swings around the Sun without falling in.
  • Put a marble in a stretched-out orbit where its speed changes by at least half.
  • Turn on Einstein's correction and get an orbit to precess a quarter turn (watch the yellow dots).

Discussion questions

  1. Count the physical degrees of freedom of the metric and explain what removes the others.
  2. Why does the Newtonian limit live in g₀₀, and what does that say about the rubber-sheet picture?
  3. With Einstein's correction on, orbits precess. Explain the origin of the 1/r³ term and connect it to Mercury's 43 arcseconds per century.

2 · Time runs slow

Missions

  • Find the distance where the clock runs at exactly half speed.

3 · Magnets have invisible hair

Missions

  • Put two charges of the same sign on the board and find the spot between them where the field is nearly zero.

Discussion questions

  1. Write Fμν in terms of Aμ and count its independent components.
  2. In what sense is electromagnetism 'already geometry' (a connection on a U(1) bundle)? On what bundle does gravitational curvature live?
  3. The energy density of the field sources gravity. Sketch how the Reissner–Nordström solution differs from Schwarzschild.

4 · Einstein's wish

Missions

  • Slide Einstein's wish all the way to 100%.

Discussion questions

  1. State Einstein's criteria for a unified field theory as precisely as you can.
  2. The Lorentz-force condition is usually the one that failed. Why must a unified theory derive equations of motion rather than assume them?
  3. What do the Coleman–Mandula and Weinberg–Witten theorems each rule out, and why did neither apply to Einstein's classical program?

5 · A hidden tiny direction

Missions

  • Zoom in and fit exactly three waves around the loop.
  • Tilt the loops past halfway and watch the ants drift as they walk.

Discussion questions

  1. Explain how a U(1) gauge transformation becomes a coordinate change in the fifth dimension.
  2. Why can the dilaton not be truncated consistently?
  3. What is the chirality obstruction in Kaluza–Klein theories, and how does string theory evade it?

6 · Twisted space

Missions

  • Move the pointer to a spot where the torsion gap is more than 20 px.

Discussion questions

  1. Define the Weitzenböck connection and show why its curvature vanishes.
  2. Why did Einstein need to break local Lorentz invariance, and what pathologies does that cause?
  3. Where does torsion legitimately appear in modern gravity (Einstein–Cartan, supergravity)?

7 · The lopsided ruler

Missions

  • Keep lopsidedness at 100% and still make both rulers agree (hint: move B straight above A).

Discussion questions

  1. Why does the antisymmetric part lack a gauge invariance, and why does that rule out a massless photon?
  2. Summarize Callaway's 1953 result and the method behind it.
  3. Where does a non-symmetric metric-like object appear in string theory, and is it electromagnetism?

8 · What we know now

Missions

  • Push the forces together.

Discussion questions

  1. In what sense is 'electroweak unification' not a true unification? (Consider the two couplings g and g′.)
  2. Why is gravity fine as an effective field theory below the Planck scale but not above it?
  3. Compare what string theory and loop quantum gravity each keep and each give up.

9 · The puzzle is still open

Missions

  2. Why does holography evade the Weinberg–Witten theorem?
  3. What is the cosmological constant problem, and why is it a clue rather than a nuisance?
